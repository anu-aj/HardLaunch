01 / The idea

Give people
a place to start.

A face and a short bio don't explain what two people might have in common. This concept brings intentions, preferences, and everyday relationship scenarios into the introduction.

The design question: how might we make dating feel more personal and easier to understand, while keeping the technology simple enough to explain in a Python assignment?

02 / The experience

A profile that
comes to life.

Choose an avatar or upload a photo, introduce yourself, set your preferences, and add your interests and optional work details. A live card shows your profile taking shape. Only long-term relationships include the 15-question quiz. Exploring connections and Casual dating go straight to review.

Each introduction comes with reasons to connect, something to explore, and an icebreaker you can copy. Between two eligible local profiles, you can start a conversation and switch to the other person to reply.

03 / A short demonstration

Two people.
One first hello.

  1. Open Profiles → Open Avery + Jordan. Create two fictional casual profiles on this laptop. They share a city, mutual preferences and interests, so both can see each other without a quiz.
  2. Open Jordan's introduction as Avery. Read what they share and copy the coffee icebreaker.
  3. Start a conversation. Paste your opening line and send it.
  4. Switch to Jordan. Read Avery's message, reply, then switch back to show the complete conversation.

Messages are saved locally. Reopening the pair preserves your edits and chat history. You can also create your own pair of profiles.

04 / Under the hood

Simple Python.
Visible reasoning.

Every flow checks city, adulthood, completed profiles, mutual gender and age preferences, and the same intention. Only long-term matches require a completed quiz and a commitment-score gap of at most one point.

Every person has up to three active, mutual connections. Long-term introductions prioritise quiz answer alignment; casual and exploring introductions use shared interests. Equal scores use a saved random tie order, and people with no match yet are considered first. Existing eligible connections stay in place, giving each conversation room to grow. A chat opens only once both people choose “I’d like to meet”. Fictional previews may fill spare spaces and cannot chat.

If you do not vibe, unmatch from the person's introduction or your conversation. The connection ends for both people and the next eligible person with a free slot can take its place. Your other connections stay. Ended chats remain available as read-only history, and the same pair will not be matched again. Blocking or reporting is always free.

Free profiles can initiate two unmatches in any rolling seven-hour period, so a person meets at most five people in seven hours. Real connections take time, so this is the rule for everyone, with no paid way around it. The app shows the remaining allowance and when another unmatch becomes available. Changing your relationship type refreshes your matches automatically, without using an unmatch, and can happen only once a month. HardLaunch is for people looking for a genuine match, so they lock in their choice.

The long-term score adds three parts: six similar areas using 10 * (1 - abs(a - b) / 3) (60 points), two shared values answers (20 points), and care fit (20 points), which rewards pairing someone who looks for reassurance with a giver. After the quiz, each person sees their attachment style. Editing a profile cannot quietly end an active match; saved conversations remain available.

A Python class represents each person. Functions, loops, lists and dictionaries handle matching and conversations. JSON file handling saves profiles, connections and messages. Automated tests check the matching, unmatching and conversation journeys. Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript provide the interface; Python needs no additional packages to run it.

The score measures answer alignment. It is not a prediction of romantic success or a psychological assessment. All sample profiles are fictional.

05 / What comes next

A small POC.
Room to grow.

This version demonstrates profile creation, photos, explained introductions, and a two-person conversation on one laptop. The profile switcher lets a presenter act as either person; it has no accounts or passwords.

A full product would need account security, privacy controls, moderation, validated matching design and a production backend. Live conversations across devices, notifications, distance-based matching and public hosting remain future work.
